# Research decision: branch coverage expansion

## Decision

Continue the sorter with a source-backed, quarantined candidate bank for twelve under-measured canonical branches:

- Christian Democracy
- Populism
- Ordoliberalism
- Anarcho-Capitalism
- Pan-Africanism
- Zionism
- Black Nationalism
- Ecofeminism
- Green Anarchism
- Islamism
- Khomeinism
- Qutbism

Each priority branch receives a starter block of four descriptive, four normative, and four prescriptive candidates. The block is an authoring and discrimination aid, not a validated scale. It has no effects, no production anchor, and no path into QUESTIONS or the scoring function.

## Why this tranche

The current workbench derives its inventory from the ontology and registry, but most catalog-only branches have no explicit respondent-facing items. The tranche covers several kinds of gap at once: host ideologies, economic variants, transnational nationalisms, hybrid traditions, ecological/anarchist intersections, a broad Islamist family, and two independently institutionalized person-associated schools. The selection therefore tests the architecture’s ability to represent scope and relation without forcing every branch under a macro parent or treating every associated tradition as a scored ideology.

The alternative of authoring every catalog-only and registry-only entry was rejected for this cycle. It would create a large undifferentiated bank, make source and neighbor review uneven, and obscure which branches are being deliberately tested. Remaining entries stay visible in the derived inventory and are explicitly deferred.

## Research guardrails

1. Use primary or authoritative scholarly descriptions as source anchors. The local source registry records URLs, retrieval dates, citation text, and a bounded supports statement.
2. Map items to existing descriptive, normative, and prescriptive facets. Do not invent a new numeric construct for an unvalidated branch.
3. Write items without target labels, party names, politicians, slogans, or current-event hooks where possible.
4. For every branch, record same-answer/different-reason risks, neighbor discriminants, false-positive audits, and remaining gaps.
5. Keep qualitative direction and centrality separate from production numeric profiles.
6. Do not run or imply respondent cognitive review. This run intentionally does not claim comprehension, retrieval, judgment, response-selection, reliability, or validity evidence.
7. A future promotion path still requires content review, neighbor distinctness, cross-cultural or jurisdictional review, and empirical validation. The existing promotion gate remains unchanged; no cognitive-review field is added.

## Source anchors used

- [Oxford Handbook of Political Ideologies](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34324) for the field’s plural and contested treatment of ideologies.
- [Abrahamian, Khomeinism](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/khomeinism/paper) for Khomeinism as a historically specific, internally contested political formation.
- [March, “Taking People as They Are”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/taking-people-as-they-are-islam-as-a-realistic-utopia-in-the-political-theory-of-sayyid-qutb/3F2E9A0BE53FF51AEA36A47249A2C12A) and [Vahdat, “An Islamic Totality”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/islamic-ethos-and-the-specter-of-modernity/an-islamic-totality-in-the-ideology-of-sayyid-qutb/9F72AF704E87E4AB60A6962661EDDDCD) for Qutbism’s political-theoretical commitments and internal totality claim.
- [Oxford Research Encyclopedia on Pan-Africanism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61663/chapter-abstract/553395327), [Cambridge on Black Nationalism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-guide-to-african-american-history/black-nationalism/13C0A82189B4F1086339C2E84BACB6A3), and [the Du Bois Review distinction between Pan-Africanism and Black separatism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/du-bois-review-social-science-research-on-race/article/abs/true-to-our-native-land-distinguishing-attitudinal-support-for-panafricanism-from-black-separatism/2378116FD1172FA43A339347603DCB11) for transnational solidarity, autonomy, variation, and neighbor boundaries.
- [Cambridge on Christian Democracy’s social capitalism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/what-is-christian-democracy/social-capitalism/8EE4DFB7697F5F05C8A63BEEA098461C) and [the Cambridge History chapter on Christian Democracy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-twentiethcentury-political-thought/christian-democracy/0E40DBC230BD418CF8E8DCEB3D06AABF) for intermediary institutions, social responsibility, and democratic pluralism.
- [Cambridge on Zionism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-political-philosophy-of-zionism/E7996C47FAD250FA1CDE77269C7DA783) and [Oxford’s Zionism: A Very Short Introduction](https://academic.oup.com/book/628) for collective self-determination and internal variation.
- [Cambridge on populism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/government-and-opposition/article/populism-in-europe-an-illiberal-democratic-response-to-undemocratic-liberalism-the-government-and-oppositionleonard-schapiro-lecture-2019/C624D1A36A8737434085C127BE310016) for thin-centered people-versus-elite claims and host-ideology distinctions.
- [Oxford on ordoliberalism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/44607) and [SEP environmental ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/) for market-order institutions, ecological ethics, and associated traditions that should not be collapsed into a single hierarchy.
