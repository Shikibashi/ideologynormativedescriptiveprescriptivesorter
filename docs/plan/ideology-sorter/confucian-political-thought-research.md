# Confucian Political Thought research tranche

Date: 2026-08-30

Status: registry-only, associated-tradition, not scored

## Research basis

This tranche treats Confucian Political Thought as a historically layered and internally contested political-philosophical field. It does not treat Confucian ethics, East Asian identity, Chinese cultural identity, family practice, educational attainment, or one modern reconstruction as interchangeable with the political-tradition label.

The source set is:

- David B. Wong, “Confucian Political Philosophy,” *The Oxford Handbook of the History of Political Philosophy*: [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34508/chapter-abstract/292826435).
- Loubna El Amine, *Classical Confucian Political Thought: A New Interpretation*: [Princeton Scholarship Online](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/18201).
- Sungmoon Kim, *Theorizing Confucian Virtue Politics: The Political Philosophy of Mencius and Xunzi*: [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theorizing-confucian-virtue-politics/CBBA00451187CBAC368D83B682D6EACC).
- “Modern Confucianism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*: [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/confucianism-modern/).
- Joseph Chan, *Confucian Perfectionism: A Political Philosophy for Modern Times*: [Princeton Scholarship Online](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/13456).
- Elton Chan, *Towards Confucian Republicanism: Democracy as Virtue Politics*: [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/59285).

Taken together, these sources support research terminology around political order, ruler–minister–people relations, ritual, moral cultivation, official conduct, welfare, and multiple classical and modern institutional interpretations. The two Chan works are bounded modern reconstructions; they are not used as definitions of the entire historical field. Kim’s Mencian/Xunzian distinction is retained as an internal disagreement rather than flattened into one ideal type.

## Ontology and scoring boundary

The target is registered as `confucian-political-thought` with registry kind `associated-tradition`.

- It is not a canonical ontology node.
- It is not a parent or subtype of Conservatism or Republicanism.
- It has no production anchor, no target-tagged production questions, and no scalar score.
- Its default governance decision is `retain-registry-only` with `source-backed-contested` evidence and `not-scored` result.
- Its twelve descriptive, normative, and prescriptive items are quarantined research candidates with no production effects.

The existing shared facets are only proxies for portions of the source field. The candidate block therefore preserves unresolved constructs such as ritual, cultivation, role relations, official selection, and the variation between classical, democratic, meritocratic, perfectionist, and republican interpretations. A future promotion would require direct construct design and the repository’s separate external review gates; this tranche does not activate either.

## Candidate and boundary inventory

The research bank adds twelve candidates: four descriptive, four normative, and four prescriptive. Their source references and layer-specific wording are defined in `src/research-bank.ts`.

Three neighbor discriminants keep the target distinct from Conservatism, Historical Republicanism, and Contemporary Neo-Republicanism. One false-positive audit guards against inferring the target from cultural identity, family obligation, hierarchy, meritocratic preference, democracy, republicanism, or familiarity with one author.

The candidates are research material, not respondent identity labels. The bank remains subject to wording, cross-cultural/jurisdictional, neighbor-distinctness, and later empirical review. No cognitive review, respondent study, psychometric claim, validity claim, invariance claim, or population-consequence claim is made here.
