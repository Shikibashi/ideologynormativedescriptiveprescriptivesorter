# WorkPM research decision — ontology coverage expansion

## Scope

This cycle continues the sorter toward ontology-wide coverage. It does not turn ideology labels into an end-state identity classifier. The working model remains compositional: descriptive beliefs explain perceived mechanisms, normative commitments express valued ends, and prescriptive commitments express institutional routes. Macro, meso, micro, hybrid, contextual, historical, and associated labels remain separate layers of interpretation.

The previous twelve-target tranche was insufficient for the active goal. The current audit found 119 research targets, only one target with a complete explicit production block, 15 scored-indirect targets, 91 catalog-only ontology nodes, and 12 registry-only entries. The next tranche therefore covers twelve core traditions and adds a governance ledger for every target.

## Source basis

- The [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/) distinguishes classical and new liberalism, private property, liberty, and variation in public authority.
- The [Oxford Handbook of Political Ideologies](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34324) and its [Conservatism chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34324/chapter/291333309) support internal ideological morphology and distinctions among moderate, reactionary, radical, and New Right conservatism.
- The [Oxford Research Encyclopedia entry on Socialism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62239/chapter/550762287), [Socialism: A Very Short Introduction](https://academic.oup.com/book/32741), and the existing welfare-regime sources support distinctions among social democracy, democratic socialism, Marxism, and communism.
- The [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Karl Marx](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marx/) supports the Marxist constructs of historical materialism, class relations, alienation, capital, ideology, and emancipation.
- The existing [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/), [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/), Sen, Ostrom, and Nozick records support the libertarian, minarchist, and libertarian-socialist boundaries.
- Existing ecological sources, including [Carter's green political thought](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/politics-of-the-environment/green-political-thought/BA5EB7C4D160DD1D16ECF39BF55C2047) and the [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Environmental Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/), support the ecosocialist boundary and the associated status of Deep Ecology and Bioregionalism.

These sources support terminology, construct boundaries, and authoring rationale. They do not validate local wording, hand-authored anchor vectors, psychometric properties, or respondent classifications.

## Selected next tranche

Classical Liberalism, Social Liberalism, Moderate Conservatism, National Conservatism, Social Democracy, Democratic Socialism, Left-Libertarianism, Minarchism, Libertarian Socialism, Ecosocialism, Marxism, and Communism.

Each receives four descriptive, four normative, and four prescriptive research candidates, a qualitative profile, at least two neighbor discriminants, a false-positive audit, and a coverage summary. The records remain quarantined.

## Explicit taxonomy decisions

- Khomeinism and Qutbism: promote to canonical catalog nodes on source-backed but contested evidence; do not score.
- Deep Ecology and Bioregionalism: demote to associated registry traditions because their source-backed status is philosophical, ecological, planning, or place-based and does not automatically constitute a political ideology.
- Every other target: receive an explicit retain, contextual, registry-only, or catalog hold decision rather than an implicit status.

## Completion tranche — 2026-08-26

The final uncovered-target pass added source-backed, research-only three-layer blocks for White Nationalism, Agrarian Populism, British Fascism, Civic Republicanism, Conservative New Right, Deep Ecology, Flemish/Belgian Fascism, French Fascism, Italian Fascism, Japanese Fascism, National-Syndicalism, Bernsteinian revisionism, and the existing Right-Libertarianism branch. The tranche contributes 156 effect-free candidates, 13 qualitative profiles, 26 neighbor discriminants, 13 false-positive audits, and 13 coverage summaries.

This closes candidate coverage at 1,428 records across all 119 ontology/registry targets. It does not close production measurement: registry entries remain outside direct scoring, catalog-only and scored-indirect nodes remain explicitly labeled, and only Right-Libertarianism has dedicated target-tagged production questions. No cognitive review, respondent evidence, simulation substitute, or psychometric claim is added.

## v6 continuation decision — 2026-08-26

The next bounded production-measurement tranche activates four existing canonical meso nodes—Libertarianism, Marxism, Social Anarchism, and Liberal Feminism—because their ontology records already provide source-bounded construct summaries, canonical placement, qualitative profiles, neighbor discriminants, false-positive audits, and editorial anchor contracts. Each receives four descriptive, four normative, and four prescriptive target-tagged prompts. The source checks use the [SEP Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/), [SEP Karl Marx](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marx/), [SEP Anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/), and [SEP Liberal Feminism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-liberal/) entries for terminology and construct boundaries only.

The same tranche changes the five broad bridge anchors—Anarchism, Green Communitarianism, Green Politics, Liberal Conservatism, and Market Socialism—to `contextual-only`. Their research records remain available, but their broad labels are excluded from production scoring to avoid treating bridge/context overlap as dedicated canonical measurement. The active release is content version 6 with 252 prompts, 20 editorial anchors, 15 canonical scoring anchors, and the full 1,428-record candidate bank still quarantined. No cognitive review, respondent evidence, substitute simulation, or psychometric validation was run.
