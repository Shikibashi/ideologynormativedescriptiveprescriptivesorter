# Research Synthesis — Ideology Layer Sorter

## Summary

The source projects point to a reusable architecture pattern: questions produce intermediate facet signals, and a separate anchor table maps those facets to interpretive traditions. Freeden's morphological account adds a useful boundary for composition: ideologies can be structured combinations of concepts, but that does not turn a composite label into a validated identity measurement. The main design risk is treating a single axis or a single nearest label as if it were a validated identity measurement.

The MVP therefore combines three separations:

1. **Claim type:** descriptive, normative, prescriptive.
2. **Measurement layer:** facets before ideology labels.
3. **Epistemic state:** answered, mixed/neutral, and no-view/missing.

## Adopted patterns

- Data-driven item effects and anchors, as seen in 8values-style projects.
- More granular internal distinctions, as seen in LeftValues and PolitiScales.
- A future adaptive question path inspired by IdeoSorter, while keeping the MVP fixed-length and auditable.
- Family-balanced display so a dense family taxonomy cannot dominate a global nearest-neighbor list.
- An explicitly gated combined pattern that averages one fit from each claim layer equally, while keeping the layer readings and cross-layer pulls inspectable.

## Rejected patterns

- One two-dimensional compass as the main result.
- A single percentage presented as identity or probability.
- Treating unanswered items as moderation.
- Copying a large external question bank without provenance and license review.
- Current party or candidate matching in the first version.
- Treating the combined pattern as an identity verdict, recommendation, or substitute for the three layer readings.

## Research-backed non-claims

- The output is a calculated similarity within this dataset, not a probability.
- A label is an interpretive neighbor, not a diagnosis or endorsement.
- A low-coverage layer is reported as insufficient information.
- Different layers may point in different directions without being called inconsistent.
- The combined pattern is available only when all three layers meet coverage; it is a local composition rule informed by multidimensional and morphological research, not a validated scale.

## v11 WorkPM continuation — Islamism

The v11 source pass selects Islamism as the most source-ready of the five remaining canonical meso holds. [Browers's Oxford Handbook chapter on Islamic Political Ideologies](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34324/chapter-abstract/291340862) distinguishes revolutionary, transnational, moderate, and post-Islamist developments. The resulting boundary is a heterogeneous modern public religious-political field in which an interpretation of Islam is relevant to organizing state or social life. The 4/4/4 item block covers public religious language, historical/structural grievance, movement institutions, constitutional variation, moral authority, social justice, self-determination, participation, accountable institutional translation, and rule-bound authority. ## v12 WorkPM research continuation — Ordoliberalism boundary

The next bounded source pass selects Ordoliberalism, an existing canonical micro node under Liberalism, rather than activating one of the four unresolved canonical meso holds. The [Oxford Handbook of Ordoliberalism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/44607) presents the term as historically specific and internally contested, with scholarship connecting it to competition as an institutional order, the rule of law, a capable state, and the social-market tradition. [Hayek's “The Use of Knowledge in Society”](https://www.aeaweb.org/aer/top20/35.4.519-530.pdf) supplies adjacent context for dispersed knowledge and limits on comprehensive direction, while the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/) supplies the broader liberal boundary. These references support construct terminology, source traceability, and original item-authoring rationale only; they do not validate the local effects, anchor vector, or respondent classification.

The source-bounded construct is a historically specific liberal-economic tradition that treats competition as an institutional order rather than merely an exchange outcome, assigns public authority a strong rule-setting and anti-concentration role, and pairs market coordination with a limited social floor. The production boundary must distinguish this combination from Classical Liberalism's broader limited-government and general-rule emphasis, Neoliberalism's wider and contested market/state reconfiguration, Social Democracy's stronger egalitarian and labor-security orientation, and Mutualism's reciprocal, possession/use, and anti-monopoly association logic. It should not infer Ordoliberalism from support for markets, anti-monopoly policy, or a social program in isolation.

The existing research record already provides 12 effect-free candidates, a qualitative profile, two named neighbor discriminants, and a false-positive audit. The v12 implementation adds four original target-tagged prompts in each of the descriptive, normative, and prescriptive layers and one provisional anchor. Descriptive items address private concentration, institutional conditions of competition, and the relation between legal order and decentralized exchange; normative items address liberty, anti-domination, social stability, and a bounded social floor; prescriptive items address competition enforcement, anti-monopoly rules, predictable law, limited ownership, and social-market provision.

## v13 WorkPM research continuation — Pan-Africanism boundary

The v13 research pass selected Pan-Africanism, an existing canonical micro node under Nationalism, for a bounded direct-coverage tranche. The [Oxford African Affairs article on Pan-Africanism as an analytical category](https://academic.oup.com/afraf/article/125/498/1/8512174) describes a contested, multi-layered category operating across institutional, social, and transnational domains and rooted in anti-imperial struggle and collective self-determination. The [Cambridge analysis of Pan-African visions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/review-of-international-studies/article/abs/internationalists-sovereigntists-nativists-contending-visions-of-world-order-in-panafricanism/85ED07FAA4CCB08F6CDB2A532437B3E2) identifies competing internationalist, sovereigntist, and racially bounded currents. The [Oxford account of Pan-Africanism and African development](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/44004/chapter-abstract/371683877) supports an anti-colonial, Black-unity, collective self-reliance, and development boundary. The [Du Bois Review study distinguishing Pan-Africanism from Black separatism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/du-bois-review-social-science-research-on-race/article/abs/true-to-our-native-land-distinguishing-attitudinal-support-for-panafricanism-from-black-separatism/2378116FD1172FA43A339603DCB11) is used to sharpen neighbor discrimination, not to validate this sorter.

The source-backed construct is therefore a transnational tradition of African and diasporic solidarity, liberation from continuing colonial or racial power, collective self-determination, and cooperation across borders. It is not one universal institutional programme: federal/continental, sovereign-state, diasporic, cultural, and economic-self-reliance emphases remain historically and strategically varied. The production wording avoids current conflicts, individual identity inference, and claims that cultural pride or state nationalism alone constitutes Pan-Africanism.

The existing research record already provides 12 effect-free candidates, a qualitative profile, two named neighbor discriminants, and a false-positive audit. A 4/4/4 block and provisional anchor are defensible as provisional editorial measurement without cognitive review, respondent review, simulations, or empirical validation. They are not defensible as a validated scale, prevalence estimate, or personal classification instrument. The selected boundary keeps Pan-Africanism distinct from Black Nationalism, Anti-Colonial Nationalism, Cultural Nationalism, and Civic Nationalism by making transnational African/diasporic scope and anti-colonial power analysis jointly visible.

Two alternatives were considered. Pan-Africanism has a strong academic source base and multiple durable visions, but its continental/transnational, sovereign, and racial-solidarity strands require a sharper scope and cross-cutting boundary before direct scoring. Black Nationalism is also source-rich, but its historically fluid relationship among racial solidarity, self-determination, separatism, anti-colonialism, and Black Feminism creates a larger false-positive and jurisdictional burden. Activating either alternative in this tranche would broaden coverage while increasing the chance that the current facet geometry collapses historically distinct projects.

Ordoliberalism is therefore activated as a provisional editorial block without a new ontology node, reparenting, demotion, new facet, target-block coefficient, or scorer change. The four unresolved canonical meso holds—Conservative Nationalism, National Socialism, Neo-Fascism, and Religious Nationalism—remain catalog-only. No cognitive review, respondent study, simulation substitute, psychometric calibration, reliability/validity estimate, invariance study, or population evidence was run or claimed.

Option A, selected, activates Islamism alone. Option B would activate Islamism together with Conservative Nationalism, but risks compounding a broad religious field with the already-nearby National Conservatism micro branch without a new dimension. Option C would retain all five holds, preserving the authority boundary but leaving the best-supported remaining canonical family without direct coverage. The addition is source-backed editorial measurement only; it does not validate the local wording, effects, anchor vector, or respondent classification.
