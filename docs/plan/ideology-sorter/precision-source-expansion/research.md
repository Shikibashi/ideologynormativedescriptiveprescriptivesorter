## Political dimensionality and ideology

1. **Treier & Hillygus (2009), “The Nature of Political Ideology in the Contemporary Electorate,” Public Opinion Quarterly, 73(4), 679–703.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/poq/nfp067) and [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/714352). The study's central measurement implication is that political preferences are multidimensional and that collapsing them can produce inaccurate conclusions. This supports layer-specific profiles and argues against treating one close label as a complete identity.
2. **Bakker, Jolly & Polk (2012), “Complexity in the European party space: Exploring dimensionality with experts,” European Union Politics, 13(2), 219–245.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1465116512436995). Expert-positioning work supports examining more than a single left/right axis and separating economic and social dimensions when the evidence warrants it.
3. **Warwick (2002), “Toward a Common Dimensionality in West European Policy Spaces,” Party Politics, 8(1), 101–122.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1354068802008001005). The paper identifies recurring left-right, social-control, and post-material dimensions in comparative party-policy data. The application uses this as a conceptual reason to add discriminating items, not as a direct respondent scale.
4. **Jost, Glaser, Kruglanski & Sulloway (2003), “Political Conservatism as Motivated Social Cognition,” Psychological Bulletin, 129(3), 339–375.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.129.3.339) and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12784934/). This is a theory review, not a recipe for classifying people. It motivates treating order, resistance to change, uncertainty, and authority as separable constructs and keeping psychological explanations distinct from policy preferences.
5. **Schwartz (1992), “Universals in the Content and Structure of Values,” Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 25, 1–65.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60281-6). This supports treating values as structured but distinct from descriptive beliefs and prescriptions. It does not license converting a value response into a political label.

## Compositional interpretation across layers

1. **Freeden (1996), *Ideologies and Political Theory: A Conceptual Approach*.** [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/3196). Freeden's morphological approach treats ideologies as structured combinations of political concepts whose meanings can overlap, transform, and regroup. This supports showing a cross-layer composition while retaining the three claim categories and their internal differences; it does not validate the local anchor profiles.
2. **Treier & Hillygus (2009).** [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/714352). The multidimensional measurement result supports preventing a high-volume or more heavily answered layer from silently dominating a combined interpretation.

The local combined-pattern rule is therefore deliberately narrow: it is withheld until all three layers meet the existing coverage threshold, computes each layer's anchor proximity separately, and averages the three layer fits with equal weight. This is a transparent display composition, not a scholarly scale, psychometric calibration, identity assignment, or recommendation. The separate layer results and cross-layer pulls remain visible so the composition can be inspected and disagreed with.

## Diagnosis of the current 99% ties

Before the previous expansion, the scorer normalized a weighted mean squared distance with `1 - distance / 4`. The preceding item bank had only 16 items per layer, several facets were represented by just two prompts, and many anchors occupied the same positive region of the hand-authored `[-1, 1]` space. A broad “Agree” response therefore sat close to multiple anchors at once. The number is a bounded similarity calculation, not a probability, but the UI made it look like a precise confidence value.

The change therefore has two separate remedies:

- expand to 24 original items per layer, with additional items aimed at economic distribution, cultural pluralism/order, democratic constraint, public provision/ownership, ecological trade-offs, and international scope; and
- retain the raw distance for deterministic ordering while exposing separation metadata and suppressing percentage-like display when the leading neighbors are not meaningfully separated.

## v10 WorkPM research continuation — conservative-school boundary

The next bounded pass selects Radical Conservatism and Reactionary Conservatism as a pair. The [Oxford Handbook treatment of Conservatism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34324/chapter/291333309) names reactionary and radical schools separately from moderate conservatism and the New Right, while also warning that the schools' accounts of political limits can be incompatible. [Pierson's Oxford treatment](https://academic.oup.com/book/40376/chapter-abstract/347086894) gives the operational boundary used here: reactionary positions seek to take back an order that has been replaced, whereas radical conservative positions resist a new orthodoxy through a radical stand. This is a historical-conceptual distinction, not a claim that contemporary respondents use either label consistently.

The [Stanford Encyclopedia's 2024 Conservatism entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/conservatism/) further supports separating Burkean/pragmatic continuity from reactionary forms and notes that conservatism is internally disputed over tradition, authority, and change. The peer-reviewed [Frontiers analysis of the conservative revolution](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2022.959411/full) documents why radical and reactionary language is historically situated and contested. The sorter therefore uses the following source-bounded construct profiles:

- Radical Conservatism: a conservative-revolutionary current diagnosing existing institutions as unable to preserve legitimate order and favoring decisive renewal of authority or common purpose. Its production boundary must exclude Fascism's additional requirements of organic anti-pluralist unity, leader-centered mass mobilization, and movement-state transformation.
- Reactionary Conservatism: a current diagnosing disorder as the displacement of inherited authority or institutions and favoring restoration, reversal, or recovered authority. Its production boundary must exclude Moderate Conservatism's gradual/corrigible stewardship and Radical Conservatism's future-facing, transformative renewal.

The existing candidate bank already contains four layer-specific indicators for each layer of both targets, with guard items for generic authority, generic tradition, Fascism, and the radical-versus-reactionary distinction. The implementation adds four descriptive, four normative, and four prescriptive original prompts for each target, plus provisional editorial anchors. The source record supports terminology, historical scope, boundary selection, and item-authoring rationale only; it does not validate local wording, effects, anchor vectors, or respondent classification.

The pair also exposes a real architecture limit: both labels share the current order/tradition and low-reform geometry, while the distinction between restored past and decisive renewal is not a first-class facet. The tranche records this as a diagnostic and preserves the existing scoring policy. It does not invent a new facet or blend target-block agreement into the score without research and calibration.
