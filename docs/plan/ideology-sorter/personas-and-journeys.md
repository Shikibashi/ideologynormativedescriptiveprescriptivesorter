# Personas and Journey Map

## Persona A — The curious self-explorer

- Goal: understand why a quiz might describe their values differently from their policy preferences.
- Concern: being assigned a label that sounds more certain than their answers.
- Need: plain-language prompts, progress clarity, no-view handling, and explanations of fit.

### Journey

1. Reads the distinction between diagnosis, values, and practice.
2. Starts the fixed-length flow and answers at their own pace.
3. Uses back navigation when a prompt feels ambiguous.
4. Reads three separate layer summaries with coverage and source notes.
5. Shares the local result link as a conversation prompt.

## Persona B — The methodology reviewer

- Goal: inspect whether an item, facet effect, or anchor is editorially defensible and traceable.
- Concern: opaque weights, copied source content, and taxonomy density.
- Need: stable IDs, source posture, version metadata, explicit scoring formula, and accessible methodology text.

### Journey

1. Opens the methodology before answering.
2. Reads the layer definitions and no-view policy.
3. Inspects question and anchor source notes.
4. Reproduces a result from a share fragment.
5. Reports ambiguous wording for later editorial review.

## Persona C — The facilitator

- Goal: use the tool as a structured discussion prompt in a classroom or reading group.
- Concern: participants interpreting a result as a verdict or recommendation.
- Need: visible disclaimer, cross-layer tensions described without shaming, and a printable/readable result view.

### Journey

1. Introduces the three-layer distinction.
2. Participants answer independently.
3. The group compares why layers can differ rather than comparing who is "more" ideological.
4. The facilitator opens the data/methodology panel to discuss editorial choices.

## Shared experience requirements

- Calm, readable density; no countdown timer.
- Every action has a descriptive label.
- No account creation or hidden answer transmission.
- Results remain meaningful when a layer is under-covered: show insufficient information.
